Paste any reply (yours or an edited draft). This flags common mistakes. It looks for words and patterns, so it can miss things and can flag harmless phrases. Use your judgment.

3. How to respond to a negative review

Respond soon, keep it short

Google recommends replying in a timely way and keeping replies short, simple, polite and professional. Long replies often don't get read.

Source: Google Business Profile Help: Tips to write better review replies.

Don't argue in public

Your reply is for future customers as much as for the reviewer. Thank them, acknowledge the problem, apologize where it's fair, and explain limits honestly without taking blame for things outside your control. Wait an hour before posting if you're upset.

Source: Google: Give helpful responses to negative reviews.

Take it offline

If it's complicated, politely ask the reviewer to contact you by phone or email so you can sort it out privately. The FTC says its review rule doesn't stop a business from contacting customers who post negative reviews to resolve the issue.

Sources: Google; FTC: Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule Q&A.

Never reveal customer information

Don't post order details, dates, what they bought, what they said on the phone, or anything else about them. Google's tips say never to share a reviewer's private information. Its content policy bans posting other people's personal information without consent.

Sources: Google tips; Google Maps content policy.

Healthcare, dental, therapy

Be extra careful. The US Department of Health and Human Services' Office for Civil Rights has settled HIPAA cases against dental practices that disclosed patient information while replying to online reviews. Don't confirm that the person is a patient, and don't mention any care, visit, or bill.

Source: HHS press release (Dec. 2022). If you're a covered entity, ask your privacy officer or attorney before replying.

No discounts for changing a review

Google prohibits offering incentives like payment, discounts, or free goods or services in exchange for posting, changing, or removing a review. Fixing the actual problem is fine. Just don't tie anything to the review itself.

Sources: Google Maps content policy; Google tips.

No false accusations or empty threats

The FTC says you can respond publicly but should "watch what you say." Its rule prohibits knowingly or recklessly false accusations about a reviewer, intimidation, and unfounded or groundless legal threats used to get a review removed.

Source: FTC Q&A, section 465.7.

Learn from it

Google notes that negative reviews aren't necessarily a sign of poor business practices, and that a mix of positive and negative feedback often feels more trustworthy. If several reviews mention the same thing, that's worth fixing. Fixing it gives you something real to say in replies.

Source: Google tips.

4. Think a review is fake or breaks the rules?

Google says you can report any review, but only reviews that violate its policies are removed. Don't report a review just because you disagree with it.

Reviews Google's policy says are not allowed (examples)

  • Fake engagement: not based on a real experience, paid for, or posted from multiple accounts by one person.
  • Conflict of interest: for example, a competitor or a current or former employee reviewing the business.
  • Personal information posted without consent, harassment, hate speech, obscenity or profanity, or offensive content such as unsubstantiated allegations of criminal wrongdoing.

Source and full list: Google Maps User Generated Content Policy: Prohibited & restricted content.

How to report it (Google's official process)

  1. Go to your Business Profile and select Read reviews.
  2. Next to the review, select Report, choose the reason (for example "Spam"), and send the report.
  3. Check the status in the Reviews Management Tool. Google says evaluation typically takes several days.
  4. If Google finds no violation and you disagree, you can submit a one-time appeal from the same tool.

Source: Google: Report inappropriate reviews on your Business Profile.

Getting a flood of 1–2 star reviews and a demand for money to remove them? Google calls this a review extortion scam. Don't engage or pay. Save screenshots of every message (with dates and sender details), and report it with Google's merchant extortion form. Google: Report negative review extortion scams.

Meanwhile, reply anyway

A calm public reply helps while a report is pending. If you truly have no record of the person, tick "We can't find any record" in step 1. The drafts will say so politely and invite them to get in touch, without calling them a liar.
